## Problem

Enterprise commerce teams were managing performance across multiple retailers, but their analytics workflow was fragmented. They needed to compare channels, build recurring reports, and understand performance without relying on slow manual work.

## My Role

I owned the product path from customer discovery through phased delivery. That included understanding reporting behavior, shaping the PRD, partnering with design and engineering, validating flows with Customer Success, and preparing the launch handoff.

## Product Approach

The important decision was to split the work into phases. The first phase focused on a clearer dashboard and report-builder experience. The second phase expanded the system toward AI-assisted insights and broader retailer coverage.

That avoided a common enterprise product trap: making customers wait for a perfect platform while their current workflow stayed painful.

## Technical Context

This was not only a UI problem. The product needed to reason across retailer data, reporting dimensions, analysis steps, and user trust. The AI layer had to sit on top of a useful analytics foundation rather than replacing it.

## Tradeoff

The tradeoff was breadth versus adoption. A narrower first release made the workflow easier to understand, while the phased plan preserved the bigger platform direction.

## Outcome

The public-safe outcome is that the product reduced customer reporting effort, strengthened the enterprise omnichannel story, and gave go-to-market teams a clearer way to sell and support the platform.
