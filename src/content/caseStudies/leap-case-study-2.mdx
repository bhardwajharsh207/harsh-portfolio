## Problem

Students had intent, but the product journey made them wait for counselor action. That created a bottleneck: motivated users were ready to move, while the operating model still required someone else to push the next step.

## My Role

I led the product shift from a counselor-pushed journey to a student-led flow. That meant challenging the operating model, piloting self-serve behavior, and working with engineering to make complex application steps easier for students.

## Product Approach

The first step was not a full rebuild. It was a controlled pilot that gave students limited self-serve capability and tested whether they would browse, compare, and act without waiting for a counselor.

Once the behavior was validated, the product expanded toward application initiation.

## Technical Context

The system needed to expose complex college and application information in a way students could understand. It also needed funnel instrumentation so the team could see whether self-serve access was creating real progress or just more browsing.

## Tradeoff

The product had to give students control without removing the value of counselors. The answer was not to replace counseling, but to remove avoidable waiting from the workflow.

## Outcome

The public-safe outcome is that the application journey became more self-serve, the counselor bottleneck reduced, and students could act closer to the moment of intent.
